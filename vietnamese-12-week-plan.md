# Vietnamese — 12-Week Study Plan (Southern dialect)

**Two tools only: LaiLingo + Migaku (lifetime).** No tutors, no podcasts, no third-party decks. Calibrated to **~45 min/day, 6 days a week**. If you only have 20 min/day, halve the new-card rate and read this as a 24-week plan — but don't cut the listening.

---

## How the two tools split the work

They're complementary, and the split is cleaner than it first looks. Migaku is not a course — it has no Vietnamese lessons or grammar instruction, and it's built for intermediate learners who can already handle authentic content. LaiLingo is the opposite: structured, frequency-ordered, with Southern audio, but it ends at B1.

| | LaiLingo | Migaku |
|---|---|---|
| Structured lessons | Yes — 71 lessons, A1–B1 | **None for Vietnamese** |
| Vocabulary order | Strict frequency rank (OpenSubtitles corpus) | You choose, from what you watch |
| Audio | Real Southern recordings (Linh San / Minh Quang) | Whatever the source content is |
| SRS | Basic | **Migaku Memory — this is your spine** |
| Sentence mining | No | **One-click from Netflix, YouTube, any web page** |
| Dictionary | No | Yes, instant lookup |
| Comprehension scoring | No | Yes — tracks known words, rates content difficulty |
| OCR (photo → card) | No | Yes |
| Speaking practice | No | **No** |

**The handoff:** LaiLingo runs weeks 1–8 and hands off to Migaku, which runs weeks 7–12 and everything after. LaiLingo gets you to the vocabulary floor where Migaku starts working. Migaku is close to useless at 200 words and excellent at 1,000.

**The bridge mechanic — do this from day one:** as you finish each LaiLingo lesson, mark those words as known in Migaku. This is what makes Migaku's comprehension score real. Once it knows your ~900 words, it will tell you which YouTube videos and Netflix titles you can actually handle, which solves the hardest problem in this setup — finding input at the right level without a graded ladder to climb.

---

## What this two-tool setup cannot do

Read this before you commit to it. Three real holes:

**1. No output, and no correction.** Migaku has no speaking or writing practice. LaiLingo's pronunciation grading was deliberately removed. So this plan produces comprehension and vocabulary, and zero corrected speech. You'll arrive at week 12 able to understand more than you can say, with tone errors nobody has ever flagged. The cheapest patch, if you ever want it: one 30-minute conversation a week. Everything else in this plan works without it — this is the one thing that doesn't.

**2. Migaku is thin for weeks 1–6.** At 250 words there is nothing on Netflix you understand at 70–90%. Migaku's own positioning is intermediate-and-up. So early on, LaiLingo's dialogues are your *only* comprehensible input, and Migaku is doing setup work: building the deck, tracking known words, waiting.

**3. Nothing here trains tones directly.** LaiLingo's Listen & Guess mode is the closest thing you have — audio-first, meaning hidden until you identify it, which is real discrimination training. Use it deliberately, not as a formality. Everything else is on you and a voice recorder.

Also worth knowing: Migaku's AI-generated definitions and auto-subtitles need checking. Vietnamese speech recognition with tone marks is error-prone, so treat auto-generated subtitles as a draft, not truth.

## What 12 weeks buys you

| Metric | Target at week 12 |
|---|---|
| Words in Migaku Memory | ~1,000 (15/day × 6 days × 12 weeks, minus attrition) |
| Coverage of everyday speech | ~80–85% |
| LaiLingo progress | Through A2, into B1 |
| Migaku comprehension score | 85%+ on at least one Southern YouTube channel |
| Listening | Follow LaiLingo B1 dialogues cold; get the gist of slow Southern vlogs |
| Speaking | Untested — see hole #1 |
| Honest label | A2 receptive |

**The math you should internalize:** 1,000 words covers 80–85% of everyday speech — roughly one unknown word in seven, about two per sentence. That's enough to speak and be understood, nowhere near enough to follow a conversation between two natives. Comfortable listening is 95–98%, which is 3,000–5,000 words, months 12–24. Feeling lost at a family dinner in week 12 is the plan working, not failing.

## The daily engine

Four things. Everything else is scaffolding.

1. **Migaku Memory — 20 min.** 15 new cards + all due reviews. Sentence cards with audio, always. Never bare word → gloss.
2. **LaiLingo — 15 min.** One lesson, in Listen & Guess mode. Do not skip to the reveal.
3. **Immersion — 10 min (grows to 25 by week 10).** Migaku extension on Vietnamese content, at whatever level your comprehension score says you can handle.
4. **Shadowing — 5 min.** Play a LaiLingo line, pause, say it out loud copying the melody. Record yourself once a week and listen back.

**Card rules:**
- Full sentence with audio on the front, one unknown word (i+1)
- Cloze the target word rather than making a bare vocabulary card
- Two unknown words in a sentence means it's above your level — don't make the card
- Migaku Memory settings: 15 new/day, cap reviews around 120
- Check Migaku's AI definition against the LaiLingo gloss when the word appears in both; if they disagree, trust LaiLingo's — it's hand-written, Migaku's is generated

## Block 1 — Weeks 1–3: Sound system and pronouns

The two things a frequency list can't teach you. Front-load them. Migaku is in setup mode this block.

**Tones.** Southern distinguishes **five**, not six — `hỏi` and `ngã` merge in speech. Drill daily: `ma` (ghost) / `má` (mom) / `mà` (but) / `mả` (grave) / `mạ` (rice seedling). Record weekly and compare against LaiLingo's native audio. You will be wrong at first and it won't sound wrong to you — that's the entire problem, and with no tutor in this plan, the recording is your only mirror.

**Southern pronunciation to internalize now:**
- `d`, `gi`, `v` all trend toward a *y* sound (`dạ` → "yah", `về` → "yeh")
- `r` stays a real *r* in the South (Northern turns it into *z*)
- Final `-n` often closes to `-ng`, final `-t` to `-c`
- LaiLingo's audio is Southern throughout — keep it that way, and pick Southern content in Migaku later

**Pronouns.** Top of every frequency list, worst-handled by flashcards. Learn the system once:

| Term | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| tôi | neutral "I" | Safe, slightly distant |
| mình | casual "I" / "we" | Friends |
| anh / chị | older man / older woman | Also means "you" — same word both directions |
| em | younger person | Both "I" and "you" |
| cô / chú | parents' age | Aunt/uncle-aged strangers |
| bác | older than your parents | |
| ông / bà | grandparent-aged | |
| con | you, to a parent-aged person | |

**The rule:** the same word is both "I" and "you"; relative age picks the pair. Get the pair right and broken grammar still sounds respectful. Get it wrong and perfect grammar won't save you.

**LaiLingo:** A1 Section I + start of II — ranks 1–270.
**Migaku setup:** install the extension and both apps, run onboarding, mark your LaiLingo words known as you go, and start building the deck from LaiLingo sentences you can type in manually.

**Gate:** hear all five Southern tones in isolated syllables at 80%+, and pick the right pronoun for a stranger ten years older without thinking.

## Block 2 — Weeks 4–6: Core frequency

The highest return-per-hour stretch of the whole year. Head down.

**LaiLingo:** A1 Sections II–III, ranks ~270–550. Cumulative ~550 words.
**Migaku:** keep marking known words. Start pointing the extension at Vietnamese YouTube even though you'll understand very little — the goal is to watch your comprehension score climb and learn which channels are within reach. Let the score, not your ambition, pick the content.
**First mined cards:** by week 6, 2–3 per day from real content, alongside your LaiLingo vocabulary.

**Gate:** you can say your name, where you're from, order food, and ask a price out loud without reading. No one will correct you — record it and judge it yourself against LaiLingo's audio.

---

## Block 3 — Weeks 7–9: Migaku takes over

The frequency list has done most of its work. From here the next word worth learning is one you actually heard.

**The switch:** cut LaiLingo-sourced cards to 8/day, add 7/day mined in Migaku. By week 9 you should be near 100% mined.

**How to mine:** while watching, when a sentence is *almost* clear except one word, one-click it. Sentence, audio, screenshot, target word. If you're pausing more than a few times a minute, the content is too hard — check the comprehension score and drop down.

**LaiLingo:** A2, ranks ~550–830 — now a supplement rather than the engine. Its grammar notes are the thing to keep reading; the vocabulary is coming from Migaku.
**Immersion:** up to 20 min/day. Southern YouTube — daily-life vlogs, street food, anything conversational. Use Migaku's auto-subtitles where the video has none, and remember they're a draft.
**Cumulative:** ~830 words.

**Gate:** 30+ cards mined from content you chose yourself, and one LaiLingo B1 dialogue understood cold on first listen.

---

## Block 4 — Weeks 10–12: Consolidation

**Cumulative:** ~1,030 words.
**Immersion:** 25 min/day, mostly native Southern content. You'll understand maybe 40%. That's the coverage math at 1,000 words, not a failure of effort.
**LaiLingo:** finish A2, start B1. Use it as your one source of *fully* comprehensible input — the confidence break from struggling through native video.
**Migaku OCR:** start photographing real Vietnamese text — menus, packaging, signs — and turning it into cards. This is the feature that pulls the language out of your screen and into the room.
**Output:** narrate your day out loud in Vietnamese while driving. Badly, with gaps. Record one two-minute monologue a week and compare it against week 1's.

**Week 12 test:**
- ~1,000 mature cards in Migaku Memory
- 85%+ comprehension score on at least one Southern YouTube channel
- A LaiLingo B1 dialogue understood cold
- A two-minute recorded monologue where you can hear your own tone errors on playback

## Tracking

Weekly, one line: `Week N — new cards: __ | mature: __ | LaiLingo lesson: __ | immersion min: __ | Migaku score: __% | gate passed? Y/N`

Miss a gate, **repeat the block**. Don't push forward. Vocabulary that outruns your ear produces someone who can read Vietnamese and understand nothing spoken — and with no tutor in this plan, nobody will tell you it's happening except you.

---

## Honest caveats

- **Syllables ≠ words.** LaiLingo counts syllables, the way the OpenSubtitles corpus does. Compounds like `máy bay` are two ranked entries for one concept, so your 1,000 is worth somewhat less than the 1,000 in Migaku's own benchmark article. Transparent compounds make up some of the difference.
- **Your corpus is movie subtitles.** Great conversational register, thin on domestic vocabulary — groceries, appointments, paperwork, kids. Migaku's OCR in Block 4 is the patch for this; point it at real-world text, not more TV.
- **Migaku has no dialect control.** It won't keep you Southern. You do that by choosing Southern channels and sticking to them.
- **Output is the known hole.** This plan is deliberately built without it. If you change your mind, one weekly conversation is worth more than any amount of additional card volume.
